The equations: LLA to ECEF

Name: geodetic to ECEF conversion

The job: turn two angles and a height into three straight-line distances from the Earth's centre.

How: the same as the globe equations on the LLA page, with a correction because the Earth is squashed. R is replaced by N, which changes with latitude, and Z shrinks a little.

a = 6,378.137 km is the distance from the centre to the equator. e² = 0.00669438 says how squashed the Earth is.

The equations: ECEF back to LLA

Name: ECEF to geodetic conversion

The job: turn three straight-line distances back into two angles and a height.

How: longitude is easy. Latitude is trickier, because the squash correction depends on the latitude we're looking for. So the computer guesses, corrects and repeats.

The answers use unrounded numbers, so a calculator may differ slightly in the last digit. The globe is drawn as a perfect ball, but every number here uses WGS84.
